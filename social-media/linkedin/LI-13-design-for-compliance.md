# LI-13. Designing for the person who signs the return

**Week 4, Friday** | Milestone: M7 | Image: `assets/screens/05-results.png`

---

Who uses a regulatory reporting tool? Someone who is personally accountable for every number, under a deadline, reading dense tables for hours.

That shaped every design choice in Daleel:

🔹 Quiet colours. Deep green and slate; colour is saved for status. Nothing animates unless it shows cause and effect.
🔹 Status is never colour alone. Every badge has an icon and a word: Pass, Warning, Breach. It works for colour blind readers and on a bad printer.
🔹 Numbers first. Tabular figures that line up, units always shown, negatives in brackets as accountants write them, and a switch between millions and full figures.
🔹 Limits next to values. "CAR 11.19%" sits beside "minimum 10.00%, 11.50% with the conservation buffer". Nobody should have to look up a threshold.
🔹 Every number is a door. Click it and a panel shows the formula, inputs, rules with their sources, and the evidence rows.
🔹 Honest labels. "Synthetic demo data" stays in the header, and every assumption says it is one.

The name came from the same place. Daleel (دليل) means evidence. In Islamic scholarship a ruling needs its daleel. So does a capital ratio.
